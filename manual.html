Manual

Fifteen lessons. The words below are the ones in the game. The pictures are from the iPhone 17e Simulator.

Career is the fifteen lessons, in order. Flight is the same pad with no mission. Sandbox is for throwing planets, and it keeps no score. On the mission board, tap a lesson, then its button. How this works opens the long page for that lesson. How to play opens the short page.

Controls

iPhone, iPad, and Mac show the same buttons. On the Mac the app is the iPad app. A keyboard and a trackpad work there too.

On the screen

Ignite lights the engines. While they are lit the same button says Cut. East and West tip the nose. Follow the green mark when the lesson says prograde: that is the way you are already going. Deploy lets a satellite go. Stage separates. During the example the assist button says Example. On your turn it says Autopilot or Autopilot on. Autopilot on says: “Autopilot has the stick. It will park near 250 × 350 km and save the ship tank.” Autopilot off says: “Autopilot off. You have the stick.” You still get the points if Autopilot flies.

The time buttons are 1×, 4×, 16×, 32×, and 64×. 1× is the speed you watch and learn at. The higher ones are fast-forward. They only change how fast the picture moves. The rocket still flies by the same rules. Next to a deorbit countdown, Fast-forward runs that wait at 128×. The last 20 seconds, and the burn, stay at 1×. Under 130 km on the way home, those time buttons stop at 16×, and a burn is still 1×.

Ship, Booster, Pad, and Orbit jump to that view. Flat sits with them on a rocket flight. It looks along the orbit normal, so the orbit lies flat on the screen, centred on Earth. Sats appears once the satellites are out. On a lesson that is not a rocket flight, the camera button reads Close up, or Orbit when the view is already close. A pinch, a mouse wheel, or a trackpad scroll up and down looks closer or wider, on a flight and on every other lesson. Two fingers slide the picture, and a sideways trackpad scroll does too. Tap the camera button to put the shot back. None of that changes the orbit. Pause stops the picture.

Close, Path, and North change how much of a long ellipse you see. Reset to pad starts the current flight again. Finished lessons and points stay, in one record on the device, aphelion-career-v1.

My turn leaves the example and starts your try. Watch, Coach, and By hand are the other lesson buttons. Retry starts that try again. Hold to burn is the voyage button. While you hold it, the label says Burning.

Keyboard

Space pauses. In a flight, A or the left arrow steers one way and D or the right arrow the other. The game flips that pair from the camera, so a positive steer tips the nose toward the left of the screen. The buttons labeled East and West are the ones that name a compass direction. W, Z, or the up arrow sets the throttle to full. Shift raises it gradually. S, X, the down arrow, or Control lowers it. Q turns prograde on or off, E radial, R retrograde. J stages, and does nothing during the example. N deploys. L launches. Plus and minus, or the numpad, change the time. On a lesson that clock stays between 1× and 64×. F toggles follow. T toggles trails.

Settings

Settings is in the menu. The lead on that page is: “The rocket flies the same path. Only the numbers you read change, and the choice stays on this device.” Units are stored on the device as aphelion-units-v1. Language is stored as aphelion-lang-v1. Changing the language does not reset the flight.

Units

Metric is the default, and it is the one the lessons are written in. Its card says: “Kilometres, metres per second, tonnes, tonne-force, and kilopascals. The lessons are written in these.” SpaceX webcast says: “Kilometres and kilometres per hour, the pair on the SpaceX vehicle tracker. Tonnes and tonne-force, the Starship page. Pressure stays in kilopascals, because that tracker does not show it.” US customary says: “Miles, or feet under one mile. Miles per hour. US tons, or pounds under one ton. Pounds of force, and psi.” The disclosure “The exact factors” lists the conversions the game uses. One foot is 0.3048 m. One mile is 1.609344 km. One pound is 0.45359237 kg. One US ton is 2,000 pounds. One pound of force is 4.4482216152605 N. One psi is 6894.757293168361 Pa. One tonne-force is 2,204.6226218487757 pounds of force. Speed in km/h is km/s times 3,600. Miles per hour are metres per second times 2.2369362920544025.

Language

Automatic says: “Follow the language of this device.” English says: “English, the words the lessons were written in.” Nederlands says: “Nederlands.” With Nederlands on, a decimal point in a number on the screen becomes a comma. Thousands stay grouped with a space.

The pictures below are the iPhone 17e Simulator with Nederlands selected, so the words on those screens are Dutch. The English names of the same controls are Metric, SpaceX webcast, US customary, Automatic, English, and Nederlands.

First orbit

Fly · 200 points · button To the pad

Goal

Park near 250 × 350 km. A little error is fine. Leave fuel in the ship — that tank is for the rest of the flight.

How the game marks it done

The ship is off the ground, above 150 km, on a closed path. Perigee is at least 220 km. Apogee is under 700 km. The gap between them is under 120 km. At least 0.8 km/s is still in the ship, and the throttle is under 0.2. A tighter cut is named Precise when the gap is under 90 km, perigee is from 220 to 450 km, apogee is at most 520 km, and at least 2 km/s is left. The points come when you finish, not when the example does.

How to fly it

  1. Tap First orbit, then To the pad. The ship waits on the pad: ALT 0.1 km, V 0. The line says: “Press Watch or Example to start.” The example button reads “Start the example” until the example is actually flying. Then it reads “Example”. TWR is 0.00 while the throttle is 0%. Watch starts it too.
  2. Press My turn. The card says: “Your turn. Park near 250 × 350 km and leave fuel in the ship. Pitch east, or Autopilot.”
  3. Go straight up, then tip east. Sideways speed is what makes the orbit. The flight strip reads “1 Burn up”, “2 Cut & coast”, and “3 Burn at the top”.
  4. Turn the engine off when the top is about 350 km, and coast. The game shows CUT NOW when that moment is here.
  5. At the top, turn the orange nose onto the green prograde ring and burn. That lifts the low side. The game shows IGNITE NOW for that burn.

For a kid

Go straight up through the thick air, then tip the nose east. Follow the green prograde mark, the way you are already going. When the high end is near 350 km, cut the engine and coast. At the top, point the nose forward and burn. That burn does not lift you higher. It lifts the low end, up to about 250 km, so the bottom stays above the air. Leave fuel in the ship. A little error is fine. Autopilot will fly this, and the points still count.

For a teacher

A Starship leaves the pad upright, then bends toward the east. The thick air is only the first minute. Where that air pushes hardest, Max Q, the engines ease off, then push again as it thins. After that, sideways speed is the whole job.

Tips

  • “Impact. The stack hit Earth — reset the pad.”
  • “Orbit, but too high: … The top must stay under 700 km. Retry and cut on CUT NOW.”
  • “Orbit, but the bottom is low: … It must reach 220 km. Burn again on IGNITE NOW, or Retry.”
  • “Orbit, but too stretched: … Top and bottom must be within 120 km. Retry and cut on CUT NOW.”
  • “In orbit, but only … km/s left. First orbit needs 0.8 km/s still in the ship.” Cut earlier, or use Autopilot.
Belly flop

Fly · 160 points · button Come home

Goal

From the air the ship comes home sideways. The belly makes the drag and the heat. Near the ground the engines flip the tail down, and the tower catches it. No legs.

How the game marks it done

The ship is home. The status then says: “Caught. … The arms have the ship. No legs on this one.”

How to fly it

  1. Tap Belly flop, then Come home. The ship is already in the air, about 78 km up at 6.93 km/s, and it waits there. The line says: “Press Watch or Example to start.” Watch or “Start the example” starts the fall. SHIP 70.0 t is propellant still in the tanks, not the dry mass. The screen says that. TWR is 0.00 while the engines are off.
  2. Press My turn. The card says: “Your turn. The ship is already in the air. Autopilot flies the belly and the flip. Watch the catch.”
  3. The ship meets the air sideways. The belly, not the nose, takes the heat.
  4. Flaps hold that belly to the wind. Engines stay off while the air does the slowing.
  5. Low down, the engines light and flip the tail under it. The tower arms catch the ship. Use 4× while it is high, and 1× for the flip. Pinch out and the whole Earth fits. Pinch back in for the ship. The flip stays on the ship. The tower comes into the frame once the ship is under about 0.42 km.

For a kid

You watch. The ship meets the air sideways. Flaps hold the belly into the wind. The engines stay off while the air does the slowing. On the way down the curved edge of the Earth and the air glow stay in view. Low down the engines light, flip the tail under the ship, and the tower arms close around it. TWR reads 0 while the throttle is 0. There are no legs. The Moon and Mars ships in these lessons use legs, because there is no tower on the Moon or on Mars.

For a teacher

Starship comes in belly-first, tipped so the wind hits the tiles. The forward and aft flaps move against each other to hold that angle. The hot air wraps the windward side.

Tips

  • The example keeps saying the engines stay off while the ship is still sideways. The flip waits until the air has done its work.
  • Watch the catch at 1×. The card on the board says: “Watch the belly, the flip, and the catch. 4× until it is low, then 1× for the flip.”
Deploy

Fly · 90 points · button To the pad

Goal

The same parking orbit, then release the satellite. Arrive with fuel still in the ship.

How the game marks it done

A released satellite whose name starts with “Sat ” is on a closed path, and its perigee is above 100 km. While the bay is open the status is: “The bay is open. Starlinks leave one by one. sped up ×10”. When one is up, the status is “Satellite on station.”

How to fly it

  1. Tap Deploy, then To the pad. Same wait as First orbit: ALT 0.1 km, V 0, and “Press Watch or Example to start.” “Start the example” or Watch starts the climb. Once the orbit is round, the bay opens and the satellites leave.
  2. Press My turn. The card says: “Your turn. Same orbit, then Deploy. Keep fuel in the ship.”
  3. Park in the same circle, with fuel still in the ship.
  4. Tap Deploy. The bay opens and the Starlinks leave one by one. Before the orbit is ready the card says “Your turn. Reach orbit, then Deploy.” In orbit it says “Your turn. Tap Deploy.”
  5. Each drifts away very slowly. Over the next orbits they spread into a line.

For a kid

Park the same circle as First orbit, with fuel still in the ship. Cut the engine. Tap Deploy once. The bay opens and the Starlinks leave one by one. Each drifts away very slowly. Over the next orbits they spread into a line.

For a teacher

On Starship the bay door opens about 20 to 35 minutes after liftoff, and a rack pushes the flat satellites out one by one, about once a minute. On Falcon 9 the whole stack is let go at once while the stage turns slowly.

Tips

  • “Sat Pe still in the air — circularize, then deploy.”
  • “The satellite went out below orbit. It falls back.”
  • “Only … km/s left — the ship can't reach orbit to deploy.”
Linger up high

Classroom · 90 points · button Ride the linger

Goal

Starts low, about 500 km. A forward burn throws the top to about 39 900 km, tilted 63.4°, so that high end sits over the far north. You are slow there, so you linger. The ellipse stays. A working satellite does not round it off.

How the game marks it done

The path has been under 2 500 km and above 36 000 km, and the craft has stayed above 32 000 km for 4 seconds. The done line is: “The ellipse stays. You lingered over the north.” There is no burn at the top. A paused example does not finish on the cue alone.

How to fly it

  1. Tap Linger up high, then Ride the linger. First you see two rings. The high one stays. The low one falls. The line says: “About 1 to 2 years at 415 km if nobody reboosts it. NASA. Higher up, the air is thinner, so it lasts longer. The low ring falling on this clock is a picture. Its exact days are UNPROVEN.”
  2. Press “Then the ellipse”. The picture stops before the low circle, before the bottom burn, and before the coast. Choose 1×, 4×, 16×, 32×, or 64×, then press Watch, Bottom burn, or Watch path.
  3. Press My turn. The card says: “Your turn. Hold the bottom burn. Linger over the north. There is no burn at the top.”
  4. Hold to burn at the bottom. The top climbs a long way up. Coast. Up high you are slow. Close, Path, and North change how much of the ellipse you see.

For a kid

Higher lasts longer. The drawn fall is a picture, and the game says its exact days are unproven. Then fly the ellipse. The lesson is finished when you have lingered over the north. The ellipse stays, about 500 × 39 900 km. A working satellite does not round it off.

For a teacher

NASA’s station FAQ of 29 September 2023 says that at about 415 km the station lasts roughly one to two years if nobody reboosts it. The page is the ISS transition FAQ. The game does not invent a kilometres-per-day decay rate. A working Molniya satellite stays on the ellipse, because the linger is the whole point. Two of them, timed apart, can keep a radio path open over the north for most of a day.

Tips

  • While you are up high and not done yet, the line is “Up high. … s — slow over the north. The ellipse stays.”
  • The last pause button is still labeled Top burn. The card says to skip that burn. The craft is named Molniya. Its short line is “lingers high over the north”.
The long ellipse

Classroom · 130 points · button Ride the ellipse

Goal

Starts as a low circle, about 250 km up. A forward burn there raises only the top, out to 35 786 km. Coast that ellipse. At the slow top, a forward burn raises the bottom until it is a circle on the GEO ring.

How the game marks it done

The path has been under 2 400 km and above 35 600 km, and the craft is now above 35 740 km. If the transfer film is still playing, its beat has to be the circle. A paused example does not finish. The card waits on Watch, Bottom burn, Watch path, or Top burn.

How to fly it

  1. Tap The long ellipse, then Ride the ellipse. The picture stops before the low circle, before each burn, and before the coast. Choose a time, then press the button: Watch, Bottom burn, Watch path, or Top burn.
  2. Press My turn. The card says: “Your turn. Hold the bottom burn, then the top burn. The coast flies itself.”
  3. Start in a low circle. Burn forward at the bottom. Only the high end rises. The button for that burn is Hold to burn.
  4. Coast the long ellipse. No burn in the middle. Close, Path, and North change the view. Leaving perigee the clock is 1× and the camera stays close. On the high arc it goes to 4×, or to the time button you picked, and never above 16×. It drops to 1× again before the top burn. Both ends are labelled with their altitudes. A green arrow on a burn says prograde.
  5. At the high end, burn forward again. The low end rises, and it becomes a circle on the gold line at 35 786 km. The same three bands as Hangs still are drawn when they fit: LEO 160 to 2 000 km, MEO 2 000 to 35 500 km, and the GEO line at 35 786 km. The lesson is finished only on the circle.

For a kid

The picture stops before the low circle, before each burn, and before the coast. Choose a time, then press the small button. After the bottom burn you can see the ship climb. The high part of that coast runs faster, then slows before the top burn. The lesson is finished only on the circle.

For a teacher

Rockets from Florida and from Kourou drop satellites onto this ellipse. Kourou is near the equator, so the plane is only tipped about 5°3'. That tip is taken out at the top, where you are slow and a sideways push is cheap.

Tips

  • On a burn the card adds “Hold to burn.” At the bottom the coach says a forward burn raises the top. At the top it says the nose forward raises the bottom.
  • GTO’s short line, on a wrong pick, is “a long, stretched ellipse”. The craft is named Transfer.
Navigation belt

Classroom · 80 points · button Ride the belt

Goal

Two burns from the 347 km parking orbit up to GPS, about 20 200 km, in six planes, two laps a day. The lesson craft is named GPS. Galileo is not a second lesson. The height-band label still names both GPS and Galileo.

How the game marks it done

MEO is selected and the height stays between 18 000 and 28 000 km for 4 seconds. If MEO is not selected the status is “Select MEO in the orbit row.” The picture of the two burns comes first. The question is after that.

How to fly it

  1. Tap Navigation belt, then Ride the belt. The example starts the perigee burn. The line says “Perigee burn. 2.03 km/s. Only the high end rises.” A gold arrow points forward, and the button says NOW while the burn is on.
  2. Coast the ellipse. Fast-forward is for the coast. The next burn waits at 1×. Press Burn forward at the top. That line says “Apogee burn. 1.41 km/s. The low end rises to the belt.”
  3. Press My turn when you want to fly it. The card says: “Press Watch, Coach, or By hand. Then you fly it.” By hand waits on the low orbit until you press Burn forward.
  4. On the question, tap MEO and hold. During the picture the top line is the climb, not a parked ring. It starts at ALT 347 km while the perigee burn is on. On the coast it rises. At the top, before the second burn, it reads ALT 20 200 km and about 2.46 km/s. After that burn it reads ALT 20 200 km, V 3.87 km/s, T 12.0 h.

For a kid

Burn forward at the bottom. Only the high end rises. Coast. Burn forward at the top. The low end rises to the belt. GPS is about 20 200 km up, in 6 planes, two laps a day. A low orbit is quicker and costs less. Higher up, a lap takes longer. Then tap MEO.

For a teacher

The two burns are a Hohmann transfer from 347 km to 20 200 km. Together they are about 3.44 km/s. GPS flies at about 20,200 km in six planes and circles the Earth twice a day. The page is gps.gov/space-segment. The GPS SPS Performance Standard, 5th edition, April 2020, Table 3.2-3, gives a semi-major axis of 26,559.8 km and an inclination of 55°. The game keeps the public 20 200 km altitude. At this sim’s Earth radius of 6 371 km and μ of 398 600.4418 km³/s², a circle there is about 3.87 km/s and the HUD period rounds to 12.0 h. The orbit row says 11 h 58 min, half a sidereal day. The craft is named GPS. Watch pays 16 of the 80 points and Coach pays 40, after the picture has flown. By hand and the question pay the 80.

Tips

  • The fuel bar turns red when the burn costs more than you have left. “This move needs 3.4 km/s.”
  • When the example finishes, the line stays: “On the belt. 6 planes, about 20 200 km, two laps a day. Spent 3.44 km/s. A low orbit is quicker and costs less. Higher up, a lap takes longer.”
  • MEO’s short line is “the middle ring, for navigation”.
Hangs still

Classroom · 120 points · button Ride GEO

Goal

35 786 km up, on the equator. One lap takes 23 h 56 min, the same time Earth takes to turn, so it hangs over one spot. The gold line is that spot. Tilt the plane and it nods north and south.

How the game marks it done

The picture has to finish on the gold line first. Then the height stays between 33 000 and 39 000 km, and the angle between the satellite and the Earth’s spin stays under 0.35 radians, for 8 seconds. The done line is: “Parked. It hangs on the gold line. One lap matches one turn of the Earth.” The example says: “It hangs on the gold line. The ground turns, the satellite stays. Press My turn to fly an engine.”

How to fly it

  1. Tap Hangs still, then Ride GEO. The picture starts on the same low circle as the rest of the classroom, about 347 km up. Pick Chemical apogee or Hall thruster before it flies.
  2. Chemical is the ArianeGroup S400-15: 425 N, 321 s, 135 g/s, MMH and N2O4. Burn forward at the bottom. Only the high end rises, out to 35 786 km. Coast the ellipse. The clock says how many hours are left to the top, about five. Fast-forward on the high arc, then 1× for the burn. At the top, burn forward again. That burn is 1.48 km/s. It rounds the path and takes out Kourou’s 5.236° tilt. The line changes from an ellipse into the gold circle. The flame and the green prograde arrow show while the engine is on.
  3. Hall is a slow outward spiral, sped up. The picture starts at 16×. The line on screen is the path already flown. EUTELSAT 172B, built by Airbus, did this with xenon Hall thrusters and 13 kW from the panels. An Ariane 5 left Kourou with it on 1 June 2017. Electric raising started on 8 June and reached this ring in four months. Launch mass about 3 550 kg. Airbus said almost six times less propellant, and up to 40% less mass. The status line counts the months, the km/s, and the xenon.
  4. On the gold line the bottom card puts the two engines side by side. The same 3 t already at GEO needs 1.80 t of chemical propellant for the top burn, 0.31 t at a 15 km/s exhaust, and 0.18 t at 25 km/s. The whole trip from 347 km is 3.89 km/s chemical, or 4.63 km/s on the spiral. For that whole trip, chemical needs 7.33 t and the slow exhaust needs 1.09 t. The 2D picture does not tilt the plane. The chemical top-burn number still includes Kourou’s tilt.
  5. From sunlight to motion: the panels make the kilowatts, the kilowatts ionise xenon, an electric field throws the ions out at 15 to 25 km/s, and the satellite is pushed the other way. Xenon is still propellant. The tank is about ten times smaller. A neutraliser lets electrons leave so the craft does not charge up. SMART-1’s Hall thruster, on a flight to the Moon, made about 68 mN from about 1.2 kW. That Moon flight is not these four months. Thrust is limited by the power. In Earth’s shadow the panels make none, so a real thruster waits unless a battery carries the burn. Airbus did not publish an eclipse fraction for EUTELSAT 172B, so this picture does not stop the spiral in shadow.
  6. Hybrid is not a third button. The split that flew is the rocket’s chemical burn, then the Hall thrusters on the satellite. A satellite carrying both an apogee engine and Hall thrusters is not given its own thrust and specific impulse here.
  7. Press My turn, pick the engine again, then Watch, Coach, or By hand. By hand, hold the burn. The coast flies itself. The long ellipse is still the two-burn transfer out to 35 786 km. It is not the navigation belt. The navigation belt is GPS, about 20 200 km.
  8. When the rings fit, the picture labels three bands. LEO is 160 to 2 000 km, with the ISS. MEO is 2 000 to 35 500 km, with GPS and Galileo. GEO is the gold line at 35 786 km, for weather and TV. Those heights follow the units and the language. On the equator it hangs over one spot. Pinch in. The ground keeps turning.

For a kid

Pick the engine, then watch the line change. Chemical is two short burns and a long coast. Hall is a spiral that keeps pushing, and it uses much less xenon, but the real flight took four months. Both end on the gold line. The card at the end is the same sums, side by side.

For a teacher

Television, some radio, and weather satellites that stare at one continent live here. A weather satellite such as Meteosat watches a whole disc of Earth without sweeping along a strip. The rocket equation is on the card: the same speed change with a slow exhaust needs much less propellant, so the electric satellite is lighter, and it takes months. The chemical satellite is heavier at launch, or it carries less payload. The picture starts at the classroom parking height, not on the Ariane transfer orbit where EUTELSAT 172B began electric raising.

Tips

  • While the 8 seconds run the line is “Hanging on the gold line.”
  • The craft is named Relay. GEO’s short line is “hangs over one spot on the equator”.
  • The chemical coast uses the same clock as the long ellipse: 1× off the bottom, 4× on the high arc, and 1× again before the top burn.
Tilt to the station

Classroom · 70 points · button Tilt to the station

Goal

408 km up, tipped to 51.6°, the same plane as the space station. Launch on that heading and you arrive with fuel to spare. Tilting a 26° orbit up to it spends almost everything.

How the game marks it done

On your turn, after the picture has flown, on the question, with the button labeled “ISS 51.6°”. That button is still the LEO orbit. The done line is “LEO. The station lives here.” A wrong pick says “That's ” plus that orbit’s name, a colon, its short line, and “Try again.”

How to fly it

  1. Tap Tilt to the station, then Tilt to the station. The example launches on the good heading. The flame points along that heading.
  2. Press My turn. The card says: “Press Watch, Coach, or By hand. Then you fly it.”
  3. By hand starts on a compass at 90°. Turn it. The line says about 41° on the ground is about 44° in space. “Launch on this heading” flies that aim. A bad aim still flies, and the picture names the wrong tilt and what it would cost to fix.
  4. “The costly way” launches due east, near 26°, then two sideways burns half a lap apart. Half a lap is about 46 minutes. Fast-forward is only on the coast. Each burn is at 1×. The line shows Δv = 2 v sin(Δi/2).
  5. When the picture finishes, the line says “You flew it. Press Question.” On the question, tap ISS 51.6°.

For a kid

Watch, then try. A launch on that heading arrives with fuel left. The costly way starts due east, near 26°, then two sideways burns. You arrive with 0.76 km/s left. You need 0.80 km/s to come home. Real crews launch into the station’s plane. A plane change works, and it costs almost all the fuel.

For a teacher

The station flies about 408 km up, tipped 51.64° to the equator. NASA. On the ground at Starbase the compass reads about 41°. In space that heading is about 44° east of north. The pure plane change from the 26° parking orbit is about 3.42 km/s, so 0.76 km/s is what is left of the 4.178 km/s the ship had. Watch pays 14 points and Coach pays 35, and only after the picture has flown. By hand, then the question, pays the 70. Tapping Watch before anything moves pays nothing. Finishing the question on Watch logs the lesson at 14. Flying By hand before that question can still make up the rest.

Tips

  • The question waits until the flight is on screen. Open the mission card on the phone if Question is folded away.
  • The word equator sits on the left of the disc. “turn here” sits on the right, on the node. They do not cover each other. On your turn the lesson card can hide the equator word.
Orbital manoeuvres

Classroom · 90 points · button Fly the burns

Goal

See what a burn does, then tilt the plane yourself. Forward and backward move the opposite end. Sideways at a node tilts the plane. Outward turns the ellipse. A plane change is possible, and it costs a lot down low.

How the game marks it done

On your turn, after you pick 5°, 10°, or 20° and the sideways burn has flown, the question asks where a plane change is cheapest. The right button is “Slow, at a node.” A banner says RIGHT ORBIT! and this lesson’s 90 points land. The status line is: “You tilted the plane. Sideways at a node does it, and it costs a lot down low. Launch into the plane you want.” Watch and Coach fly the tilt for you and do not ask. A wrong button stays on the question.

How to fly it

  1. Tap Orbital manoeuvres, then Fly the burns. The example plays four burns, then a 10° tilt. Each burn is about six seconds. Fast-forward does not speed this picture.
  2. Forward at the bottom. The high end rises and the low end stays 347 km. The gold arrow points forward and is named Prograde. The bar says this move needs 0.4 km/s. That is the 0.41 km/s burn that would raise the far end from 347 km to 2 000 km. Then the arrow turns backward and the high end falls. That half does not print a second cost.
  3. Forward at the top. The low end rises and the high end stays 2 000 km. That burn is 0.39 km/s. Then backward, and the low end falls toward 800 km. The two forward burns together are 0.80 km/s.
  4. Sideways at the node. AN and DN mark the two crossings of the equator. The line through the disc tilts. Normal points up, then Anti-normal points down and the tilt comes back. Ten degrees costs 1.34 km/s at 347 km and 1.20 km/s at 2 000 km.
  5. Outward, then inward. The dashed ellipse turns in the plane. The low end stays 800 km and the high end stays 2 000 km. The line says the size barely changes. There is no fuel bar on this burn.
  6. The example then flies 10° sideways at 347 km. The line counts the degrees, the km/s spent, and what is left of the 4.178 km/s the ship had. At 10° the pure tilt is 1.34 km/s, so about 2.84 km/s is left. The picture rounds that to 2.8. The disc reads 10.0° and the gold bar is shorter.
  7. The last line of the example is the comparison. Launch into the plane you want. Walking the node is the same sideways burn. 28.5° down low costs 3.79 km/s. At the slow top, 0.79 km/s alone, or 1.83 km/s with the circle. From Kourou, 5.2° with the circle is 1.48 km/s.
  8. Press My turn. The card says: “Press Watch, Coach, or By hand. Then you fly it.” By hand plays the same four burns, then three buttons: 5° · 0.67 km/s, 10° · 1.34 km/s, and 20° · 2.68 km/s. Coach flies 20°. Watch flies 10°. After your burn, tap Slow, at a node.

For a kid

Forward lifts the far end. Backward brings it down. Outward turns the oval without really changing its size. Sideways, where the orbit crosses the equator, tips the whole plane. You can see the line tilt. It costs a lot when you are low and fast. Do it up high, or launch into the plane you already want.

For a teacher

A pure plane change at a node is Δv = 2 v sin(Δi/2). Vallado, Fundamentals of Astrodynamics and Applications. The same relation is the simple plane change in the FAA handbook, Maneuvering in Space, section 4.1.5. A due-east launch from Kennedy is given there as 28.5°. The game uses that angle for Cape Canaveral. Kourou is 5.236°, and the line rounds it to 5.2° while the sum uses 5.236°. Combining the tilt with the circularising burn is the cheaper apogee manoeuvre. Howard D. Curtis, Orbital Mechanics for Engineering Students, chapter 6, works the same comparison: the plane change at the slow top costs less than the same tilt in low orbit. At 347 km the game’s circle speed makes 5° cost 0.67 km/s, 10° cost 1.34 km/s, and 20° cost 2.68 km/s. The drawn tilt is that pure change at 347 km. It is not the combined apogee burn. The picture is the existing globe, looked down from the pole, with the ground track drawn thicker. It is not a new 3D engine. The top line during the picture is the ship’s own parking readout, about 410 km, not the heights printed on the ellipse. Watch pays 18 of the 90 points and Coach pays 45, after the picture has flown. By hand and the question pay the 90.

Tips

  • The question’s other buttons are “Fast and low” and “Anywhere on the circle.” Neither one finishes the lesson.
  • Twenty degrees at 347 km is 2.68 km/s of the 4.178 km/s on board, so about 1.50 km/s is left.
  • The long ellipse is still the two-burn transfer out to 35 786 km. The navigation belt is still GPS at about 20 200 km. This card does not move either of them.
Polar

Classroom · 70 points · button Watch the poles

Goal

817 km up, about 101 minutes a lap, straight over the north pole and then the south. The line stays put while Earth turns, so the next pass is a new place.

How the game marks it done

The picture starts at Vandenberg, heading south. It is marked done when Polar is selected, the path has crossed a pole line at least four times, and the Earth’s spin has passed 0.7 radians. If Polar is not selected the status is “Select Polar in the orbit row.”

How to fly it

  1. Tap Polar, then Watch the poles. The picture opens on a compass at Vandenberg. Watch launches south. The arrow beside the disc points down and is named South. N is the top of the disc and S is the bottom. A gold track grows from the pad down to the south pole. The line says Earth spins east at 0.38 km/s and this heading takes none of it.
  2. Press My turn. The card says: “Press Watch, Coach, or By hand. Then you fly it.”
  3. By hand starts at 90°, due east. Turn left and Turn right move the compass by 15°. South is about 183°. Launch on this heading. Due east is not over the pole, and the line says so.
  4. When the picture finishes, press Question and tap Polar. The path goes over the north pole, then the south pole. Your line stays still. Earth turns underneath, so the next lap sees a new place.

For a kid

South from Vandenberg. Earth spins east, and a south launch gets none of that push. The track runs toward the pole, over the ocean. Then tap Polar. EAST slides around the rim because the Earth is turning, not because the satellite is chasing the ground.

For a teacher

Vandenberg is 34.74° N. The inertial azimuth is 180°, due south, so the pad’s eastward speed along the track is 0. Due east at the same pad is 0.38 km/s of that spin, and the plane stays near the site latitude. The compass reads 183° because the pad is already moving east. Watch pays 14 of the 70 points and Coach pays 35, after the picture has flown. By hand and the question pay the 70. The question is unchanged: Polar selected, four pole crossings, and the Earth’s spin past 0.7 radians.

Tips

  • The craft in this lesson is named MetOp. The long page also says MetOp really flies 98.7°, sun-synchronous, so it keeps the same morning light.
  • A wrong pick names the orbit you tapped. Polar’s short line is “straight over both poles”.

How the game marks it done

Sun-sync is selected, the path has crossed a pole line at least four times, and the Earth’s spin has passed 0.7 radians.   2. Press My turn. The card says: “Press Watch, Coach, or By hand. Then you fly it.”

For a kid

Set the tilt a little past the pole. Earth’s bulge, not the engine, keeps that plane with the Sun. The wrong tilt drifts off the day-night line. Then tap Sun-sync.

For a teacher

Tips

To the Moon

Voyage · 120 points · button Leave Earth

Goal

Forward at Earth raises the far end to the Moon. Coast. Forward at the Moon raises the near end until you share the Moon's path around Earth. Orbiting the Moon itself is a different burn: backward, at the closest pass.

How the game marks it done

The voyage is done when its beats are finished. The steps on screen are “1 · Earth, forward”, “Coast”, and “2 · Moon, forward”. The lesson text says it is done only when both burns are finished. This lesson stops on the path that travels with the Moon. The backward burn that orbits the Moon is the next one, and this lesson does not require it.

How to fly it

  1. Tap To the Moon, then Leave Earth. The picture stops before each burn and before the coast. Choose a time, then press Earth burn, Watch coast, or Moon burn.
  2. Press My turn. The card says: “Your turn. Hold to burn at each burn. The coast flies itself.”
  3. Point forward at Earth. The far end rises out to the Moon. Hold to burn.
  4. Coast for about five days. The Moon comes to meet that far end. The coast button is Watch coast. On your turn a coast can also wait.
  5. Point forward again. You travel with the Moon. The button is Moon burn.

For a kid

The picture stops before each burn and before the coast, with the line that says what comes next. Choose a time, then press the small button. Press My turn when you want to fly the burns yourself. The coast does not burn. In this picture that coast is about five days. The lesson is done only when both burns are finished.

For a teacher

Apollo and Artemis light a long burn in low Earth orbit and then coast for about three days. Small corrections tidy the path. At the Moon a backward burn drops the ship into orbit around the Moon. Only after that does a lander go down. This picture is the slower coast, out to the far end, about five days. Apollo meets the Moon sooner.

Tips

  • While a burn is waiting, the status ends with “Hold to burn.”
  • The example coast runs at least at 16×. The time chips still work. A burn stays on the clock you picked.
Burn home

Voyage · 130 points · button Return

Goal

You are traveling with the Moon. Slowing down is the way home. Backward at the Moon drops the near end onto Earth. Coast for about five days. Backward at Earth drops the far end, and you stay on an oval.

How the game marks it done

The voyage is done when its beats are finished. The steps are “1 · Slow at the Moon”, “Coast · about 5 days”, and “2 · Slow at Earth”. The lesson ends when the second burn has dropped the far end.

How to fly it

  1. Tap Burn home, then Return. Choose a time, then press Slow at Moon, Watch coast, or Slow at Earth.
  2. Press My turn. The card says: “Your turn. Slowing down is the way home. Hold the backward burn at the Moon, then at Earth.”
  3. Point backward. Slowing down drops the near end toward Earth. Hold to burn.
  4. Coast home for about five days. Engines off. The path is already aimed.
  5. Point backward at Earth. The far end drops. You finish on an oval, not a circle.

For a kid

The picture stops with a line before each backward burn and before the coast home. Earth, the Moon, and the path stay in one view, so you can see the near end fall. Choose 1× when you want it slow. The coast is the long part, about five days in the lesson clock. Press My turn when you want to hold the burns yourself. The lesson ends when the second burn has dropped the far end.

For a teacher

Apollo lit the engine once, behind the ship at the Moon, and fell toward Earth for about three days. The air did the last of the slowing: a hot entry, then parachutes. There was no second burn at Earth, because the plan was to land, not to stay in orbit.

Tips

  • Pointing the nose at Earth and thrusting does not drop you onto it. Thrust along your path is what changes the far end. The lesson says slowing down is the way home.
  • In this lesson there is no air-catch, so you fly that second backward burn yourself and remain on an oval.
To Mars

Goal

Same two burns, Sun in the middle. Forward at Earth raises the far end to Mars. Months of coast. Forward at Mars raises the near end until you travel with Mars.

How the game marks it done

After the burn at Mars the picture continues. The game marks the voyage done on a flyby past Mars, or when the entry has landed. The steps before that are “1 · Leave Earth”, “Coast · months”, and “2 · Arrive at Mars”. The lesson text says it is done when the burn at Mars has raised the near end.

How to fly it

  1. Tap To Mars, then Leave for Mars. Choose a time, then press Leave Earth, Watch coast, or Arrive at Mars.
  2. Press My turn. The card says: “Your turn. Hold forward at Earth, then at Mars. The months of coast fly themselves.”

For a kid

For a teacher

Tips

Home from Mars

Voyage · 160 points · button Come home

Goal

Backward at Mars drops the near end onto Earth. Coast. Backward at Earth drops the far end. You then share Earth's year. A capture into low orbit would be one more backward burn, or a dip into the air.

How the game marks it done

The voyage is done when its beats are finished. The steps are “1 · Slow at Mars”, “Coast · months”, and “2 · Slow at Earth”. When you finish it yourself, the status adds “Earth is here.” The watched example’s last frame on the phone does not keep a mission line. The lesson ends when the second burn has dropped the far end. The phone then reads about 0.00 AU from Earth, 29.86 km/s, and a period of 368.0 days.

How to fly it

  1. Tap Home from Mars, then Come home. Come home on this card is this lesson, not Belly flop.
  2. Press My turn. The card says: “Your turn. Slowing down is the way home. Hold the backward burn at Mars, then at Earth.”
  3. Press Slow at Mars and hold Hold to burn. Slowing down drops the near end toward Earth.
  4. Coast home for months. The button on the coast is Watch coast. Engines off. The path is already aimed.
  5. Press Slow at Earth and hold the backward burn. You share Earth's year around the Sun.

For a kid

The picture stops with a line before each backward burn and before the months of coast. The Sun, Mars, and Earth stay in one view, so you can see the near end fall. Choose 1× when you want it slow. The coast is the long part. Press My turn when you want to hold the burns yourself. The lesson ends when the second burn has dropped the far end.

For a teacher

A crew leaves Mars on that same ellipse, coasts for months, and meets Earth at the near end going faster than Earth. One more slowing, almost always by the air, finishes the trip. The window matters again: Mars and Earth have to be in the right places, or the near end misses.

Tips

  • This lesson ends when you share the year. A low orbit around Earth would be one more backward burn, or a dip into the air.
  • The points are awarded once, on your turn, when the lesson is really finished. Doing it again does not add points.
