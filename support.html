Aphelion support

A single-player gravity game. Fourteen lessons, then a sandbox.

Each lesson, the buttons, and the check the game uses are in the manual.

How do I reset progress?

Reset, on the flight bar, starts the current lesson again. Finished lessons and points stay. Those live in one record on the device, aphelion-career-v1. Deleting the app removes that record. There is no account to delete.

What does each chapter teach?

Fly has three lessons. Watch the rocket, then fly it. The board order is First orbit, then Belly flop, then Deploy.

  • First orbit. Park a low orbit and leave fuel in the ship.
  • Belly flop. Come home sideways, flip, and meet the tower.
  • Deploy. The same parking orbit, then release the satellite.

Classroom has eight lessons. Fly the picture, or watch it, then pick the orbit. Hangs still asks for an engine first: a chemical apogee burn, or a Hall thruster that spirals up. The long ellipse is still the two burns out to 35 786 km. Orbital manoeuvres sits between Tilt and Polar.

  • Linger up high. A low ring falls sooner than a high one. Then the long ellipse, slow at the top.
  • The long ellipse. A low burn raises the far end, then a burn at the top rounds it.
  • Navigation belt. Two burns from the low orbit up to GPS, about 20 200 km, in six planes.
  • Hangs still. Pick a chemical apogee engine or a Hall thruster, then climb to 35 786 km. One lap matches a turn of the Earth, so the craft holds one spot.
  • Tilt to the station. Aim the compass, or turn a 26° orbit up to 51.6°.
  • Orbital manoeuvres. Forward and backward move the far end. Sideways at a node tilts the plane. Pick 5°, 10°, or 20°.
  • Polar. Over the north pole, then the south.
  • Dawn-dusk. Launch a little past the pole, near 98.5°, so the plane stays on the day-night line.

Voyage has four lessons. Watch both burns, then do them.

  • To the Moon. Raise the far end to the Moon, then match its path.
  • Burn home. Slow down at the Moon, then at Earth, and stay on an oval.
  • To Mars. The same two burns, with the Sun in the middle.
  • Home from Mars. Slow down at Mars, then at Earth, and share Earth’s year.
